Australia on Fire

Exploring a decade of bushfire activity across Australia and the world — from global patterns to local rainfall, and the fires burning right now.

Chapter 1

A Global Problem

Bushfires are not unique to Australia — they burn across every continent. This chapter establishes the global context, identifying where fire activity is most intense and how it has changed over the past decade.

Active fire detections were unevenly distributed across the world from 6–15 May 2026. Australia recorded the highest fire activity, while India, Russia, Angola, Mexico and China also showed high numbers of detections. Many countries in Europe, North Africa and South America had relatively low fire activity.

Australia recorded the highest number of high-confidence fire detections in 2026, followed by Russia, Mexico and India. The top countries show that bushfire activity was highly concentrated in a small number of regions rather than evenly distributed worldwide.

Global fire detections fluctuated between 2014 and 2024, with no steady upward or downward trend. The highest level occurred in 2023, followed by a sharp decline in 2024. This suggests that global fire activity varies strongly by year.

The heatmap shows that fire activity was consistently high in the Democratic Republic of Congo and Brazil from 2014 to 2024. Australia and Canada show especially high fire detections in 2023, suggesting that fire activity can vary sharply by country and year.

Fire detections changed unevenly between 2014 and 2024. Brazil and Angola increased, while the Democratic Republic of Congo, Bolivia and Indonesia showed large decreases. This highlights strong country-level differences in long-term fire activity.
