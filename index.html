Global Bushfire Activity

Bushfires are one of the most destructive natural hazards on Earth. This visualisation explores global fire patterns from 2014 to 2024, with a focus on Australia — examining historical trends, the role of rainfall, and current fire conditions.

Chapter 4 — The Role of Rainfall

Annual Rainfall by Australian State/Territory (2014–2024)

Area-averaged rainfall across six states from 2014 to 2024. The red dashed line marks 2019, when rainfall hit its lowest point across nearly all states — coinciding with the Black Summer bushfire crisis.

Rainfall vs Bushfire Detections (2014–2024)

Each point represents one state in one year. Triangles mark 2019. States with lower annual rainfall tend to record more fire detections — the negative correlation is strongest in NSW and South Australia.

Bushfire & Rainfall Deviation from Average (2014–2024)

Orange bars show how much fire activity deviated above or below the 11-year average. Blue bars show the same for rainfall. In 2019, fire activity was 37% above average while rainfall was 42% below — the starkest divergence in the dataset.
