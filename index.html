Australia on Fire

Exploring a decade of bushfire activity across Australia and the world — from global patterns to local rainfall, and the fires burning right now.

Chapter 1

A Global Problem

Bushfires are not unique to Australia — they burn across every continent. This chapter establishes the global context, identifying where fire activity is most intense and how it has changed over the past decade.

Global Active Fire Detections

This map shows fire detection intensity by country for May 2026 using NASA VIIRS satellite data. Darker colours indicate more fire detections. Central Africa and South America consistently record some of the highest global fire activity.

Top 20 Countries by Bushfire Activity

The twenty countries with the highest number of high-confidence fire detections from NASA VIIRS. Australia is highlighted in dark red among the most fire-prone nations on Earth.

Global Fire Detections Over Time (2014–2024)

Annual global fire detections from 2014 to 2024. The peak in 2023 reflects widespread fire activity across multiple continents, while 2022 saw a notable dip linked to La Niña conditions bringing wetter-than-average weather globally.

Country-Year Fire Detection Heatmap (2014–2024)

Annual fire detections across the top 10 countries from 2014 to 2024. Darker cells indicate higher fire activity. Australia's dramatic spike in 2019 — the Black Summer — stands out clearly against its otherwise moderate fire years.

Change in Fire Detections by Country (2014 vs 2024)

This dumbbell chart compares fire detections in 2014 (light orange) and 2024 (dark red) for the countries with the largest absolute changes. The gap between the two dots shows how dramatically fire activity shifted over the decade.

Chapter 2

Australia: A Decade of Bushfires (2014–2024)

Zooming in on Australia, this chapter traces ten years of fire activity across states and territories — revealing which regions burn most, how the pattern has shifted over time, and the catastrophic scale of the 2019–20 Black Summer.

Bushfire Detections by Australian State

State-level fire detection totals across Australia in 2024. The Northern Territory and Western Australia account for the largest share of detections, driven by extensive tropical and semi-arid vegetation.

Share of Fire Detections by State (2014–2024)

Each bar shows how each state contributed to Australia's total annual fire detections. The Northern Territory dominates most years. In 2019, New South Wales surged dramatically — its share more than tripling compared to a typical year.

Annual Bushfire Detections by State (2014–2024)

Each panel shows the fire detection trend for one state or territory from 2014 to 2024, using a shared Y-axis so the scale of each state's fires can be directly compared. The Northern Territory consistently records the most activity.

Australia Total Fire Detections Over Time (2014–2024)

National fire detection totals from 2014 to 2024. The 2023 peak reflects a severe fire year across the Northern Territory and Queensland, while the wet La Niña years of 2020–2022 suppressed fire activity significantly across the country.

Chapter 3

Current Fire Conditions (2026)

Moving from the historical record to the present day — where are Australia's fires burning right now, and how intense has the current season been?

Fire Intensity Across Australia

Bubble locations show where fires have been detected recently across Australia. Bubble size represents fire detection count; colour represents average Fire Radiative Power (FRP), a measure of fire intensity. Larger, darker bubbles indicate the most severe fires.

Daily Bushfire Detections in Australia (March–May 2026)

Daily fire detection counts over the most recent two months. Two significant peaks are highlighted — 8,064 detections on 15 April and 8,154 on 7 May — suggesting an unusually active autumn fire season for 2026.

Chapter 4

The Role of Rainfall

What drives Australia's fire seasons? Rainfall — or the lack of it — is one of the most powerful predictors of fire activity. This chapter draws on Bureau of Meteorology rainfall data to reveal the link between drought and fire.

Annual Rainfall by Australian State/Territory (2014–2024)

Area-averaged rainfall across six states and territories from 2014 to 2024. The red dashed line marks 2019, when rainfall crashed to its lowest point across nearly all states — the driest conditions in decades, directly preceding the Black Summer catastrophe.

Rainfall vs Bushfire Detections (2014–2024)

Each point represents one state in one year. Triangle markers highlight 2019. Dashed trend lines are shown for the four states with the strongest negative correlation — lower rainfall clearly associates with more fire detections, most strikingly in New South Wales and South Australia.

Bushfire & Rainfall Deviation from Average (2014–2024)

Orange bars show how much national fire activity deviated above or below the 11-year average. Blue bars show the equivalent for rainfall. In 2019, fire activity was 37% above average while rainfall was 42% below average — the starkest divergence in the dataset, and the year that defined the Black Summer.
